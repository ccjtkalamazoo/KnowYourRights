Know Your Rights · CCJT

PRIVACY

This game does not know who you are. There is no account, no login, and nothing to fill in. It records which answers people choose so we can find out which rights are most often misunderstood, and it does that without collecting anything that identifies you.

Not collected. Ever.

What this game never records

  • Your name, email, address, or phone number
  • Any account or login, because there isn't one
  • Your IP address
  • A device ID, cookie, or fingerprint
  • Your location
  • Anything you type, because there is nowhere to type
  • The date or time of day you played
  • Any link between one visit and the next

Section 1

What it does record

When you play, the game creates a random session number. It is generated the moment you start, is never stored on your device, and disappears when you leave. Attached to that number, it records:

  • Which question appeared
  • Which answer you chose, and whether it was correct
  • How many times you have seen that question in this session
  • How long you spent, measured as a length of time rather than a clock reading
  • Whether you changed your answer before locking it in
  • Which lifelines you used, and in what order
  • Whether you read the review cards, and for how long
  • Where you stopped, if you left partway through
  • Whether your screen is phone-sized, tablet-sized, or larger
  • Whether sound was on or off

Our server adds one more thing: the month. Not the day, not the time. Just the month, so that old records can be found and deleted on schedule.

Section 2

Why we record it

The session number and the answers attached to it are used only to run and improve this game. Specifically:

  1. To find out which rights are misunderstood. If most players believe police must read Miranda rights at the moment of arrest, that is a gap worth teaching to, and the only way to see it is to count answers.
  2. To check the game works. Whether people get stuck on a confusing question, whether something breaks on small screens, whether anyone finishes.
  3. To share what we learn, as group totals only. A finding like "38% of players thought X" contains no session numbers and no individuals. Nothing is published unless at least 100 people have answered that question.
The session number is never used to contact anyone, to build a profile of anyone, or for advertising of any kind. This game carries no ads and sells nothing. CCJT does not sell, rent, or trade any of this data.

Section 3

How long it is kept

These records are kept for as long as they are useful. Nothing in them identifies a person: there is no name, no device identifier, and no way to connect one visit to another, so a record from three years ago says exactly as little about who you are as one from today.

What they do hold is which answers people chose, and that becomes more useful with age. Whether understanding of these rights improves over a decade is a question you can only answer by keeping the earlier years to compare against.

  • Kept Answer records, which carry only a session number that was discarded when you closed the game
  • Kept Group totals, which describe no individual at all

Section 4

How it is protected

The strongest protection here is what was never collected. There is no name to leak, no address to expose, and no way to work backward from a record to a person. Beyond that:

  • Records are held in a private database controlled by CCJT. There is no public way to read them.
  • The address that receives game data accepts new records only from this game's own website, and cannot be used to read anything back.
  • Access to the account holding the data is limited to the CCJT team and protected by two-factor authentication.
  • Published findings are group totals only, and are withheld entirely for any question answered fewer than 100 times, so that no small group can be singled out.

The CCJT team is responsible for this program, for reviewing these risks at least once a year, and for updating this page when practices change.

Section 5

Questions, or a request

If you have a question about any of this, or need to review a data agreement before using the game, write to ccjtkalamazoo@gmail.com.

You can see exactly what your own session has recorded at any time: open your browser's developer console while playing and run KYR_EVENTS.dump(). It prints the full list. We would rather show you than ask you to take our word for it.
